# Sentence revision and argument diagnostics

The failure sentences below identify problems. Revisions demonstrate language and reasoning; they are not verified thesis evidence. Actual delivery requires real Findings references and source support; design examples require implementation records. Adapt them to their paragraph context. Do not apply one set of sentences to all four dimensions.

The later [participant-context document](participant-context.md) supplies additional facts and quotation variants. The examples below retain earlier illustrative wording; before using them in thesis prose, reconcile it with the current source. In example A, “washing my own body” differs from the supplied English “washing your own body”. In example C, B07 is interview only. B08's features in D are now reported by the user, although visitor outcomes still require separate evidence.

## A. A vague relationship and lost context

**Failure:** “B06 developed a particular embodied relationship with her home.”

**Missing:** Particular has no named object. Readers cannot tell what she did or why the room became familiar. The participant's own bodily metaphor has disappeared.

**Revision approach:** Retain the experiential chain when first introducing this case, then explain how the actions developed familiarity. The conversation's material supports the following illustrative structure, subject to verification against Findings:

> B06 lived alone in a small loft. She had no mop and used a bucket and a small towel to clean the floor, bathroom surfaces, corners and grout by hand. Repeated kneeling, reaching and wiping brought her into close contact with the room's surfaces. She described her body and the room as having “grown together” and compared cleaning to “washing my own body.” Her familiarity with the room developed through these repeated acts of touching and cleaning.

**Check:** The final sentence is grounded in the preceding actions. If the argument concerns fidelity, continue by explaining the touch–familiarity connection to preserve during translation and comparing it with B08. This passage supplies an empirical anchor; it does not yet complete the Discussion contribution.

## B. Adjacent cases without a comparative inference

**Failure:** “B08 described darkness. B06 described cleaning. Both examples demonstrate the importance of embodied experience.”

**Missing:** Darkness loses invisible boundaries and certainty of enclosure; cleaning leaves familiarity unexplained; the conclusion could apply to any bodily activity.

**Comparative reasoning after necessary context has been established:**

> B08 located enclosure in the certainty of a boundary whose shape she could not see. B06 described familiarity through repeated contact with the room's surfaces. The comparison identifies two forms of continuity to consider during translation: restricted visibility and enclosure for B08, and repeated touch and familiarity for B06. Affective fidelity therefore requires the designer to identify what makes each remembered situation recognisable in the participant's account.

**Check:** The final sentence follows from two explicitly different connections. Continue with actual design records showing how I addressed each continuity. Do not simply add a theorist's name and declare the dimension proven.

## C. Theory used only to confirm a general proposition

**Failure:** “Pink argues that sensory experience is situated. B07 and B04 confirm this. This informed my multimodal design.”

**Missing:** Situated is not specified in either case; comparison and decisions are absent; the study's further translation problem remains unstated.

**Revision action:** Within an argument that has already positioned the contribution, explain B07's external sound source entering a private room. Explain how B04's white noise, bodies, light, action/waiting belong to one event. Compare the configurations. Cite Pink accurately to explain the situated character of sensing. Then identify which connections I selected across media and how I actually organised them.

**An intermediate inference to examine:**

> B07's loudspeaker connected a shared residential space with the private room in which she heard it. B04's white noise belonged to the bodily and visual conditions of a testing scene. Translating these accounts required me to identify the connections through which each sound acquired meaning.

**Check:** These sentences still need verified theoretical claims and actual decisions in the surrounding argument. They cannot replace that work. Do not invent sound technologies or visual effects from these clues.

## D. The researcher disappears and design consequences remain unclear

**Failure:** “B08's account became an invisible boundary in VR. This relationship informed the design.”

**Missing:** The decision-maker, reason, and change are unclear. The implementation of an invisible boundary is unspecified.

**Use only if design records confirm weak light and difficult-to-see geometry:**

> I used weak light to support orientation and kept the surrounding geometry difficult to see. I made these choices to retain B08's connection between limited visibility and the certainty of enclosure. The lighting gave visitors some visual orientation within a space whose surrounding geometry remained difficult to discern.

**Check:** The passage names implementation and intention. Visitor fear or identification with the participant requires additional evidence. If implementation is unverified, identify the missing design record in the editorial notes; do not present the example as completed work.

## E. The sentence ends before the thought is complete

**Failure:** “The translation process did not end after the workshop. Couldry discusses voice.”

**Missing:** Readers must guess who did what after the workshop, how it concerns voice, and why Couldry belongs here.

**Revise according to documented actions:**

> After the workshops, I continued to interpret participants' words, drawings and sensory descriptions as I developed their accounts into spatial and interactive forms. My choices about colour, sound, boundaries and interaction shaped which parts of each account visitors could encounter.

**Continue:** Develop one documented choice and explain how it organised the account's presentation. Connect Couldry's precise argument to that now-explicit interpretive responsibility. The two sentences alone do not establish that voice was preserved.

## F. Defensive writing, overclaiming, and corrective opposition together

**Failure:** “I do not claim to reproduce experience. Rather, my study transforms participatory design by establishing not just representation but a new paradigm of embodied memory.”

**Problems:** The opening anticipates an objection; Rather extends the negation-and-replacement pattern; transforms/new paradigm exceeds the evidence; no specific research result appears.

**A bounded claim based on the B08/B06 comparison:**

> My comparison of B08 and B06 identifies different connections to preserve during immersive translation: limited visibility and enclosure for B08, and repeated touch and familiarity for B06. These connections specify what affective fidelity needs to address in each account.

**Check:** A clear contribution survives the removal of inflated claims. The argument does not invent a weak alternative to elevate its conclusion. Extend the passage through evidence, explanation, and decisions; avoid grander adjectives.

## G. Manufacturing an agency argument through opposition

**Failure:** “Agency is not about how far visitors move, but about meaningful action.”

**Problems:** The structure is prohibited; meaningful action names no concrete conditions.

**Revise after establishing B02/B04's context:**

> B02's changes in bodily position altered how she encountered the same room. B04's movement across boundaries changed the actions available to her. The comparison directs attention to the position, boundary and permission conditions that give an action meaning in each account.

**Check:** Continue by specifying the actions and conditions I actually designed for visitors. Without implementation evidence, do not claim an interaction was completed.

## H. Chinese silently supplies reasoning missing from English

**Misaligned pair:** English says “My choices shaped the space.” Chinese adds “我通过弱光保留不可见边界与封闭感的联系，因此访客能体验参与者的恐惧”. The Chinese adds a lighting decision, its rationale, and an asserted visitor experience of fear.

**Revision action:** Check for records of the weak-light implementation. If documented, both languages must explain the choice, reason, and observable encounter conditions. Without visitor feedback, remove the claim that visitors experienced the participant's fear. Both versions must distinguish design intention from observed results.

**Sentence-level alignment fields:** Who acts → what they act on → which material supports the action → which conditions result → how strong a conclusion follows. Deliver only when the two versions agree on each field.
